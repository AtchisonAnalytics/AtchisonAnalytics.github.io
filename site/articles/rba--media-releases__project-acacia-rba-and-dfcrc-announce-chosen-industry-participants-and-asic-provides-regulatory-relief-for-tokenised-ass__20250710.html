Project Acacia: RBA and DFCRC announce chosen industry participants and ASIC provides regulatory relief for tokenised asset settlement research project

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) and the Digital Finance Cooperative Research Centre (DFCRC) have revealed the industry participants selected for the Project Acacia pilot, a major research initiative exploring the settlement of tokenised assets. The Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC) has granted regulatory relief to support the project’s testing phase, facilitating innovation in digital financial infrastructure.

Project Acacia aims to investigate the potential for blockchain technology to streamline the settlement of tokenised financial assets. By simulating a real-world environment, the project will assess operational, legal, and regulatory challenges within Australia’s payments and securities sectors. This signal from the RBA and DFCRC stresses their commitment to fostering digital finance innovation while ensuring regulatory compliance and market integrity.

The chosen participants span a range of financial institutions and technology providers, reflecting the collaborative nature of the initiative. Their involvement will provide practical insights on how tokenised asset settlement could fit within existing market frameworks. ASIC’s regulatory relief specifically eases certain licensing and compliance requirements, enabling participants to test the system without facing standard regulatory constraints. This enables a controlled and safe exploration of novel technologies while protecting market participants.

This support aligns with the RBA’s broader strategic interest in advancing the payments ecosystem, including digital currencies and distributed ledger technologies. Recent RBA publications highlight the increasing importance of digital asset infrastructure for enhancing efficiency and inclusivity in financial markets. The involvement of the DFCRC underscores the project’s research intensity and focus on cooperative industry innovation.

While the project is experimental, its outcomes could inform future policy and regulatory adjustments in Australia’s financial sector. If successful, tokenised asset settlement might reduce costs, improve speed, and increase transparency in securities trading and clearing processes. However, the project also recognises the need to address potential risks, including legal uncertainty and operational resilience.

In announcing the pilot and associated relief, the RBA, DFCRC, and ASIC reinforce their roles as enablers of responsible innovation. They seek to strike a balance between supporting emerging technologies and maintaining the robustness of Australia’s financial system. The pilot’s findings will be closely monitored, with a view to shaping practical frameworks that can harness the benefits of tokenisation without compromising investor protection or financial stability.

Project Acacia thus represents a forward-looking, collaborative step towards modernising Australia’s financial market infrastructure. By providing a secure environment for testing and learning, it charts a path for the evolution of tokenised asset settlement, with potential benefits for market participants and the broader economy.
