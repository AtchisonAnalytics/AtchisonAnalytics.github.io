APRA Executive Board Member Suzanne Smith – Speech to the Members Health Directors’ Professional Development Program

APRA emphasises the critical role of directors in strengthening resilience against information security incidents, including cyber-attacks, to protect the financial sector’s stability. Suzanne Smith, an Executive Board member, outlined directors’ governance responsibilities and forthcoming regulatory enhancements at the Members Health Directors’ Professional Development Program on 4 February 2025.

APRA’s recent tripartite reviews examined the entire regulated population and revealed areas needing improvement across multiple organisations. Smith highlighted that the evolving threat landscape demands continuous vigilance, rigorous oversight and sustained investment to manage cyber risks effectively. She underscored the necessity for swift, effective responses to breaches to safeguard entities and their stakeholders.

Directors must fulfil specific obligations to enhance governance. According to Smith, these include clearly defining the organisation’s purpose, values and desired risk culture, then ensuring these are consistently upheld. She stated that leaders should “set the tone from the top and ensure it cascades through the entity.” This top-down approach is fundamental for embedding robust risk management and accountability.

Smith also insisted directors adopt a self-critical stance and demonstrate openness to external benchmarking of practices. “You should be open to acknowledging and responding to gaps,” she said. Directors must rigorously challenge management by verifying that the information they receive is accurate and based on sound data. This demand for scrutiny reflects APRA’s increased supervisory focus on governance this year.

APRA plans to release a discussion paper proposing changes to its core governance prudential standards across all regulated industries soon. These proposed reforms signal a tightening of expectations on boards to oversee risk culture, data integrity and incident preparedness more effectively.

The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority supervises a broad swathe of the financial services industry, including banks, mutual funds, insurance companies, private health insurers and superannuation funds, overseeing approximately $9.8 trillion in assets. This vast responsibility means APRA’s governance standards profoundly influence Australia’s financial system stability.

Smith’s address sought to reinforce that robust governance is not merely regulatory box-ticking, but a vital factor in managing increasingly sophisticated cyber threats. By ensuring directors lead with clarity and rigor, institutions can better protect depositors, policyholders and fund members.

In sum, APRA is signalling a steeper supervisory approach combined with updated governance standards to keep pace with evolving risks. Directors within APRA-regulated entities should expect heightened scrutiny of their oversight functions and prepare to deepen their engagement with risk culture and information integrity. This evolving regulatory landscape demands continuous improvement and board-level accountability to maintain public confidence in Australia’s financial system.
