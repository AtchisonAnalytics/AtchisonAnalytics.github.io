Review of Merchant Card Payment Costs and Surcharging

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has initiated a review of merchant card payment costs and surcharging practices. This review aims to assess whether current fees and charges remain appropriate for merchants and consumers, reflecting evolving payment system dynamics.

The RBA’s review focuses on the charges merchants incur for accepting card payments and the extent to which these costs are passed on to consumers through surcharges. Merchant service fees, which merchants pay to card issuers and payment networks, have historically influenced the final cost faced by consumers when they choose particular payment methods. The review seeks to determine whether these fees fairly reflect the costs merchants impose on the payment system and whether surcharging practices align with regulatory expectations.

The RBA has a longstanding role in supervising card payments markets to promote efficiency, competition, and transparency. By evaluating merchant service fees and surcharging, the Reserve Bank intends to ensure that merchants and consumers are not subject to excessive or unjust charges. This includes scrutiny of whether surcharges are proportionate to actual payment processing costs and whether they affect consumer behaviour or merchant pricing strategies unduly.

While the review’s detailed findings and recommendations are yet to be released, its timing coincides with rapid changes in payments technology. Contactless payments, mobile wallets, and new payment platforms have altered card usage patterns and cost structures. The RBA’s analysis will likely consider these technological shifts alongside regulatory measures introduced to govern surcharging, such as caps on excessive fees and requirements for transparency.

Historical experience indicates that merchant service fees and surcharging can have significant economic effects. Excessive fees may discourage card acceptance or lead merchants to inflate surcharges beyond reasonable cost recovery, imposing undue burdens on consumers. Conversely, appropriate fee levels and fair surcharging can encourage efficient payment choices, benefiting the economy at large.

According to previous RBA statements, the Reserve Bank supports reforms that promote fair competition and reduce unnecessary costs in payment systems. This review forms part of the Bank’s broader mandate to oversee Australia's payment ecosystem and protect consumer interests.

In ensuring merchants and consumers receive accurate cost signals, the review will inform future policy and regulatory settings. Its conclusions may lead to adjusted fee frameworks or enhanced guidelines on surcharging practices, fostering transparency and fairness.

The RBA’s ongoing commitment to this review signifies its intent to adapt regulation in line with payment market developments. For merchants, consumers, and industry stakeholders, the outcome will clarify expectations around card payment costs and surcharging, potentially reshaping transactional relationships in Australia’s payments landscape.
