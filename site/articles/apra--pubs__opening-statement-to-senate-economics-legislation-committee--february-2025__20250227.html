Opening Statement to Senate Economics Legislation Committee – February 2025

APRA has affirmed that Australia’s financial system remains resilient and stable, supported by robust regulation and proactive risk management. This assurance is significant amid ongoing economic challenges and emerging risks such as geopolitical tensions and technological developments.

The 2025 environment for financial services is described as complex and uncertain. APRA highlights that institutions must anticipate risks linked to cost-of-living pressures, global economic interconnectivity, and advances in artificial intelligence. While AI offers potential benefits in efficiency and customer service, it also carries unforeseen risks that require careful oversight and management.

Recent floods in Queensland, which caused substantial property losses, underscore insurance’s critical role in financial security. APRA emphasises that addressing the protection gap will demand sustained collaboration between industry, government authorities at all levels, and other stakeholders. This effort aims to enhance resilience against natural disasters and reduce financial exposure for policyholders over the long term.

APRA stresses its commitment to using its full enforcement powers to mitigate serious prudential risks within regulated entities. The regulator recently imposed conditions on the superannuation fund Cbus due to identified concerns over governance practices, specifically relating to fit and proper processes and expenditure management. Similar licence conditions were applied to BUSSQ, though this decision faces a Federal Court appeal after a previous judicial review was dismissed.

The statement notes that well-governed institutions tend to be more resilient during periods of financial stress. This observation supports APRA’s focus on ensuring entities maintain strong governance frameworks and prudent practices. Effective governance is presented as a key factor in sustaining stability and protecting the interests of depositors, policyholders, and superannuation fund members.

APRA supervises a broad array of financial institutions, including banks, mutuals, insurers, and superannuation funds that collectively hold roughly $9 trillion in Australian assets. This extensive responsibility positions APRA as a central figure in safeguarding the integrity and soundness of the nation’s financial infrastructure.

In sum, APRA’s opening statement to the Senate Economics Legislation Committee delivers a clear message: despite current and emerging challenges, the financial system is stable due to comprehensive regulation and proactive risk management. The regulator remains vigilant in enforcing standards and adapting to new risks to maintain community confidence and financial security.
