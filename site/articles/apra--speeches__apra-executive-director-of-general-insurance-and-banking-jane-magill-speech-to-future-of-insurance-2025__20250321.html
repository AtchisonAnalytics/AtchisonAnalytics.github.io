APRA Executive Director of General Insurance and Banking Jane Magill speech to Future of Insurance 2025

Jane Magill, Executive Director of General Insurance and Banking at the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA), outlined the regulator’s role and oversight scope at the Future of Insurance 2025 conference on 21 March 2025. Her remarks highlighted APRA’s critical responsibility in maintaining the stability and integrity of Australia’s financial services industry, which holds nearly $10 trillion in assets for depositors, policyholders, and superannuation members.

Magill emphasised APRA’s broad supervisory reach across key financial sectors, including banks, mutuals, general insurance and reinsurance companies, life insurers, private health insurers, friendly societies, and the superannuation system. This diverse oversight ensures that institutions operate prudently and remain resilient to risks that could threaten financial security for Australians. She noted that APRA currently supervises entities managing approximately $9.8 trillion in assets, underscoring the significant scale and complexity of its regulatory mandate.

The speech also reinforced APRA’s commitment to rigorous prudential standards that safeguard financial stability while supporting industry innovation and customer outcomes. Magill described how APRA adapts its supervisory approach to evolving risks posed by changes in the economy, technology, and consumer behaviour, aiming to foster a robust and trusted insurance sector.

By underscoring its comprehensive supervisory framework, APRA signals its intent to balance prudent risk management with flexibility in a shifting financial landscape. This approach is integral to protecting both individual policyholders and the broader economy from shocks, ensuring the long-term sustainability of Australia’s insurance and banking systems.

Magill’s address at Future of Insurance 2025 serves as a reminder that APRA remains vigilant in its oversight duties, with a focus on stability and resilience at a time of rapid industry transformation. For Australians, this translates into confidence that their deposits, insurance policies, and retirement savings are protected under stringent prudential supervision.
