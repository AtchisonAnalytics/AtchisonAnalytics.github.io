APRA Executive Board Member Suzanne Smith - Remarks to the ICA conference 2025

APRA Executive Board Member Suzanne Smith emphasised the critical role of insurers in managing rising risks and futureproofing Australia during her address at the ICA conference on 10 October 2025. She called on the insurance sector to maintain resilience, embrace innovation, and demonstrate strong leadership to sustain public trust and the industry’s social licence.

Smith outlined how insurers face an increasing array of challenges including geopolitical instability, cyber threats, and the impacts of climate change. Despite these pressures, community expectations remain clear: insurers must fulfil their fundamental obligation of managing claims fairly and transparently. She urged the sector to focus on what is within their control—running their businesses prudently, managing risks effectively, and communicating openly with customers to uphold trust.

Resilience, according to Smith, remains the "heartbeat of insurance". She highlighted industry progress in disaster planning and implementation of the Industry Action Plan following the 2022 Flood Inquiry as positive steps. However, she stressed resilience is a continuous process requiring solid fundamentals such as a strong capital base, prudent underwriting, operational resilience, robust claims management, and cyber security adaptive to evolving threats. APRA expects insurers to proactively strengthen these basics and comply with the Financial Accountability Regime (FAR), which since March 2025 has designated individuals accountable for key insurance functions including capital and risk management.

Innovation, Smith declared, is not optional but essential for futureproofing insurance and supporting competitiveness. She pointed to technology advances like digital tools and artificial intelligence (AI) as drivers of efficiency in areas like underwriting, claims, cybersecurity, fraud detection, and crisis prevention. Smith stressed that innovation must always prioritise the customer through transparency, especially around premium changes and their drivers, to build trust and business value.

Leadership in a risky world demands courage and clarity, Smith said. She referred to APRA’s latest Insurance Sector Risk Culture Survey, which identified good practices such as engaged boards, constructively challenging executives, and a culture where staff feel safe to speak up. The survey also revealed weaknesses, particularly among claims and technology teams, requiring urgent attention. Addressing these issues can enhance insurers’ competitive position and the sector’s overall resilience.

Smith noted APRA’s commitment to balance prudential safety with regulatory efficiency, as outlined in the regulator’s 2025 Corporate Plan. APRA plans to reduce unnecessary burden through initiatives like adjusting reinsurance settings for cost-effective access, reviewing data collection practices, and exploring data sharing with government agencies to minimise duplication and complexity. It will consult with industry to find the best regulatory balance, inviting feedback on duplicative or overly complex requirements.

In closing, Smith emphasised that futureproofing entails anticipating challenges and mitigating shocks without expecting insurers to predict every emerging risk. She reiterated that public expectations for fair treatment are enduring, and insurers must uphold fundamental responsibilities to maintain trust and their social licence. If insurers master these basics, innovate thoughtfully, and lead with strong risk culture, they will not only withstand future shocks but also strengthen the sector’s indispensable role in Australia’s economy and communities.

Smith’s address framed 2025 as a pivotal year for Australian insurance, urging the sector to seize the opportunity amidst risks to prepare for a resilient, innovative, and accountable future.
