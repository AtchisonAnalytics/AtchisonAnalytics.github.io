Financial Stability Review - October 2025

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has released its Financial Stability Review for October 2025, affirming that the Australian financial system remains resilient despite elevated global risks. This assessment matters as it highlights the system’s capacity to endure shocks while cautioning on vulnerabilities from international uncertainty and evolving financial sectors.

According to the RBA, Australian households are experiencing eased budget pressures, aided by falling inflation and interest rates. Many borrowers have built savings buffers through offset and redraw accounts, keeping mortgage arrears at a low level. Nonetheless, the report notes that financial strain persists for some, pointing to ongoing challenges in household cash flow management.

Businesses in Australia are described as broadly resilient although conditions remain difficult for certain sectors. Insolvency rates have stabilised around their longer-term averages, but some industries face higher stress. The RBA expects cash flow pressures on companies to diminish further, although the economic outlook retains considerable uncertainty.

The Australian banking system is assessed as strong, with institutions well positioned to support the economy in a downturn. The RBA emphasises the continued importance of sound lending standards to maintain financial system health. Banks’ capital and liquidity buffers remain adequate to absorb losses if necessary.

Risks from other financial institutions such as superannuation funds, non-bank lenders and insurers are currently contained. The rapid growth of superannuation funds poses a future challenge, requiring ongoing efforts to strengthen resilience. Non-bank lenders, though a smaller part of the financial system, are growing and warrant close monitoring for emerging risks.

Elevated uncertainty characterises the global financial environment, where unexpected events could trigger sharp market moves. The report points to the digital transformation within the financial system as raising operational risk, including potential incidents that might disrupt services or markets.

Overseas shocks are identified as a significant external risk to Australia’s financial stability. A negative shock could sharply increase financing costs for businesses and financial institutions. Exporters and related suppliers would be vulnerable to a major trade disruption in any of Australia’s key trading partners, with repercussions for financial market stability.

The RBA’s half-yearly review underscores that, while the domestic system is robust, heightened global financial volatility and structural financial changes require vigilance. Maintaining sound lending practices, monitoring growth in non-bank lenders, and preparing for operational risks amid digital shifts remain crucial priorities.

In sum, the Financial Stability Review signals confidence in Australia’s financial system resilience but warns that external shocks and evolving sectoral dynamics could still pose notable challenges ahead. The report advises continuous attention to risk management to safeguard economic stability.
