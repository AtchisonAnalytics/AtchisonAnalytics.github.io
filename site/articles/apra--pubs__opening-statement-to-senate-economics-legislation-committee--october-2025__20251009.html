Opening Statement to Senate Economics Legislation Committee – October 2025

The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) presented its opening statement to the Senate Economics Legislation Committee on 9 October 2025, emphasising its role in overseeing the stability of Australia’s financial system. This testimony matters as it outlines APRA’s continued commitment to protecting the interests of depositors, policyholders, and superannuation fund members.

APRA regulates a wide range of financial entities, including banks, mutuals, general and life insurers, private health insurers, friendly societies, and most superannuation providers. According to APRA, it currently supervises institutions managing approximately $9 trillion in assets on behalf of Australian consumers. This vast scope highlights the authority’s significant responsibility to maintain prudential standards that safeguard the financial health of these organisations.

The statement underscores APRA’s focus on ensuring the resilience of supervised institutions amid ongoing economic and financial challenges. By enforcing rigorous capital and risk management requirements, APRA aims to reduce the likelihood of financial failures that could harm individuals and the broader economy. The regulator also plays a critical role in monitoring systemic risks that could arise from interconnectedness within the financial sector.

APRA’s comprehensive supervision framework helps bolster confidence in Australia’s financial services industry by promoting sound governance and operational practices. In so doing, the authority contributes to the stability and efficiency of the market, which supports economic growth and consumer protection simultaneously. This approach ensures institutions remain capable of meeting their commitments to customers, even under adverse conditions.

In conclusion, APRA’s opening statement to the Senate committee reaffirmed its pivotal role in overseeing a vast and complex financial system. By maintaining strict prudential regulation and risk oversight, APRA acts to protect millions of Australians who rely on these institutions for their financial security now and into the future.
