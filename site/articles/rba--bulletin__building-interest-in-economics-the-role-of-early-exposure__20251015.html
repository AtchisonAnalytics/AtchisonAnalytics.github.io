Building Interest in Economics: The Role of Early Exposure

The Reserve Bank of Australia’s 2024 survey reveals that early exposure to Economics through the Years 7–10 Commerce elective boosts student interest and understanding of the subject. This matters because it highlights a pathway to improving perceptions of Economics in senior secondary education and addressing socio-economic disparities in participation.

According to the RBA’s survey of students in New South Wales, Economics remains widely seen as relevant and beneficial to society. However, the appeal of studying Economics in Years 11 and 12 is less favourable compared to earlier schooling years. The 2024 data provides new evidence that integrating Economics as a core topic within the Commerce syllabus introduced in 2019 has helped foster this early engagement. Students who completed the Commerce elective in Years 7 to 10 reported higher levels of confidence and interest in Economics.

The analysis shows the greatest gains were among students from lower socio-economic backgrounds, who are traditionally under-represented in Economics. This signals that early compulsory exposure can help close participation gaps and diversify the cohort pursuing Economics in senior years. The Commerce curriculum’s inclusion of mandatory Economics content appears to provide foundational knowledge that encourages students to consider Economics positively before reaching Years 11 and 12.

These findings imply that education policy and school curriculum design should prioritise providing more students with access to Economics during their early secondary school years. Expanding opportunities to engage with Economics at this stage may foster a broader and more inclusive interest in the discipline, leading to improved perceptions and higher enrolment rates at senior levels. For students across diverse socio-economic backgrounds, such early exposure represents a critical touchpoint to build confidence and sustained interest.

In sum, the RBA’s 2024 survey underscores that embedding Economics early in secondary education plays a key role in shaping how students view the subject later on. Emphasising early Commerce involvement can promote equitable participation and help redress the less favourable attitudes towards Economics found in senior secondary education. This approach offers a practical route to strengthening Economics education and engagement among Australian students.
