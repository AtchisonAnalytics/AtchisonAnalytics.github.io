A New Measure of Job Searchers for Australia

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has developed a more comprehensive measure of job searchers to better gauge spare capacity in the labour market. This innovation addresses limitations in the traditional vacancy-to-unemployed ratio, improving the understanding of labour market imbalances.

The standard measure compares job vacancies to the number of unemployed persons, aiming to capture the gap between labour demand and supply. However, the RBA highlights a key flaw: it assumes that only unemployed individuals fill job vacancies. In reality, vacancies are also filled by other job searchers who may not be classified as unemployed, such as those outside the labour force or individuals searching while still employed. This discrepancy can misrepresent true labour market tightness.

To overcome this, the RBA has constructed the Searchers Index, which integrates both the unemployed and non-unemployed job searchers. By broadening the definition of those actively seeking work, the index reflects a more nuanced picture of labour supply. The introduction of the Searchers Index enables the calculation of a vacancies-to-searchers ratio, an alternative to the traditional vacancies-to-unemployed ratio.

This new ratio shows different properties from the original measure and is expected to provide a sharper signal of spare capacity. The vacancies-to-searchers ratio accounts for the broader pool of potential workers who are likely to fill vacancies, leading to a more accurate assessment of labour market conditions. Consequently, this ratio will join the existing set of indicators the RBA uses to evaluate spare capacity.

The inclusion of the Searchers Index and its derived ratio represents an important development in labour market analysis. It acknowledges the complex reality of job search behaviour and encourages more informed policy decisions. With this enhanced measure, the RBA will better detect imbalances between labour demand and supply, enabling more precise assessments of underlying economic conditions in Australia.
