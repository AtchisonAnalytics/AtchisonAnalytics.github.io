Statements on Monetary Policy

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) released its latest Statement on Monetary Policy on 12 August 2025, setting out its assessment of the Australian economy and the outlook for inflation and employment. This statement guides expectations on interest rates and monetary policy settings, which are critical to financial markets, businesses, and households.

The RBA reports that the Australian economy continues its moderate expansion amid ongoing global uncertainties. Economic growth is currently supported by solid consumption and strengthening business investment, while export conditions remain generally favourable. However, the Bank notes that productivity growth has slowed, presenting a challenge to raising living standards and containing inflation pressures.

Inflation in Australia remains modestly above the 2–3 per cent target range, primarily driven by higher costs in housing and services sectors. Wage growth has picked up slightly but remains subdued overall, limiting upward pressure on inflation. The RBA expects inflation to remain within the target range over the medium term, assuming continued moderate growth in wages and stable commodity prices.

The labour market remains tight, with the unemployment rate at historically low levels and participation rates rising. Employment growth has been concentrated in services sectors, while some sectors face labour shortages. The Bank emphasises the importance of this tight labour market in underpinning future wage increases, which are necessary to sustain inflation within target.

Against this backdrop, the RBA's monetary policy stance is cautious but accommodative. The official cash rate has remained steady, reflecting the Bank’s judgment that current settings are appropriate to support sustainable growth and inflation. The RBA flags that it will continue to monitor economic developments closely, particularly wage dynamics, inflation trends, and global risks, to determine the future path of interest rates.

The statement highlights risks to the economic outlook, including persistent global geopolitical tensions and disruptions to supply chains which could weigh on growth and inflation. Conversely, stronger-than-expected domestic demand or wage growth could prompt earlier monetary tightening. The Bank acknowledges the delicate balance it must maintain to achieve its inflation target without undermining employment.

In assessing these factors, the RBA reiterates its commitment to transparency and clear communication, signalling changes to policy well in advance when necessary. The current assessment suggests monetary settings will remain stable for the foreseeable future, barring significant shifts in economic conditions.

For business and consumers, this statement indicates a period of relative monetary stability supporting ongoing investment and spending while keeping inflation expectations anchored. Continued vigilance is warranted as the Bank navigates complex domestic and international factors influencing the economic landscape.

Overall, the RBA’s Statement on Monetary Policy underlines a cautiously optimistic outlook. Sustainable economic growth appears feasible, but challenges from slower productivity and external risks will require careful policy calibration in the months ahead. This careful stance aims to maintain low inflation and high employment, essential for Australia's economic wellbeing.
